Welcome! This integration visualizer is designed to make integration clear and highly interactive.

How to Control and Interact

  • Drag Limits of Integration: Grab the highlighted circular indicators of a or b on the horizontal axis and drag them left or right to change bounds dynamically.
  • Navigate the Graph: Click and drag your mouse/touch inside the coordinate plane to pan. Scroll your mouse wheel or use the zoom buttons to inspect curves up close.
  • Presets Selection: Use the shortcut preset buttons in the sidebar to load classic calculus functions.

Understanding Riemann Sums

A Riemann sum is an approximation of the definite integral. As the number of subintervals $N$ increases ($N \to \infty$), the Riemann sum converges toward the exact definite integral value:

Left & Right Sums: Uses the function's value at the left or right boundary of each interval block to construct the height of approximating rectangles.
Midpoint Sum: Evaluates the function at the midpoint of each interval block. Generally exhibits lower error than left or right approximations.
Trapezoidal Rule: Approximates the area under the curve using trapezoids instead of rectangular columns, which hugs curve slopes exceptionally well.
Signed Area: Positive regions (above the x-axis) are shaded in green, adding positive value, while negative regions (below the x-axis) are shaded in red, representing negative contributions to the integral.
